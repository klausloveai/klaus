# Field map — I-140 (EB-1A self-petition)

Edition **06/07/24**, 8 pages, 286 AcroForm fields. Verified 2026-08-20 against
`uscis.gov/i-140` (page Edition Date 06/07/24, footer 06/07/24 — they agree) and
by rendering every page of the blank form. Re-verify with
`uscis_forms.py fetch i-140` before reuse.

Prefix for every name on page *N*: `form1[0].#subform[N-1].` — e.g. page 2 fields
are `form1[0].#subform[1].<name>`. The tables below give the bare name.

F = FIXED firm data · S = self-petitioner (= beneficiary on an EB-1A) · D = dependent.

## Why the names never match the printed numbers

The AcroForm names were carried over from an older layout, so on this edition they
are systematically offset from what is printed on the page. Four separate offsets,
all confirmed by rect:

| Block | Offset | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Part 3 "Other Information" (p2) | names run **+2** | printed **3.** Date of Birth = `Line5_DateOfBirth` |
| Part 3 last-arrival block (p2) | names run **−1** | printed **11.a** I-94 number = `Line14a_ArrivalDeparture` |
| every address block | letters offset | printed **3.a** street (p1) = `Line6b_StreetNumberName` |
| Province / Postal Code, all blocks | **swapped** | printed **3.g** Province = `Line6h_Province` |
| printed Parts 8–11 (p6–p8) | prefix runs **−1 Part** | printed **Part 8** = `Part7_*` fields |

Resolve anything not listed here by rect with
`uscis_forms.py dump forms/i-140.pdf --pages N`, against the rendered page — never
by reading the field name.

### Part 2 — Petition Type (item 1 continues onto page 2)

One checkbox, `prt2PetitionType`, eight members:

| Export | Index | Printed option |
|---|---|---|
| **`/a`** | `[1]` | **1.a. An alien of extraordinary ability ← EB-1A** |
| `/b` | `[2]` | 1.b. An outstanding professor or researcher |
| `/c` | `[3]` | 1.c. A multinational executive or manager |
| `/d` | `[4]` | 1.d. Advanced degree / exceptional ability, **not** seeking NIW |
| `/e` | `[0]` | 1.e. A professional (bachelor's or foreign equivalent) |
| `/f` | `[7]` | 1.f. A skilled worker (≥2 years) — page 2 |

| `/h` | `[6]` | 1.h. An alien applying for an NIW — page 2 |

EB-1A is always `/a`. The indices are scrambled relative to the printed order —
set the value on the member whose `on=` state matches, which for `/a` means the
field `prt2PetitionType[1]` on page 1.

`Line2a_ReceiptNumber[0]`; 2.b Schedule A `Line2_Petition[1]`=`/S`. Both blank on
a normal EB-1A.

### Part 3 last-arrival block — names run −1, and it is conditional

**Firm rule.** Fill items 10–11.c only if the beneficiary is inside the US.
Otherwise item 11.a reads `Not in U.S.` and 10, 11.b, 11.c stay blank. Items
12–15 are filled either way.

| Printed | Field | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 10. Date of Last Arrival | `Line13_DateOArrival[0]` | I-94 admission date |
| **11.a. Form I-94 Arrival-Departure Record Number** | `Line14_I94Number[0].Line14a_ArrivalDeparture[0]` | **comb field, `MaxLen 11`** |
| 11.b. Expiration Date of Authorized Stay | `Line14e_ExpDate[1]` | I-94 "Admit Until Date" |
| 11.c. Status on Form I-94 | `Line15_CurrentNon[0]` | I-94 class of admission, e.g. `F1` |
| 12. Passport Number | `Line14b_Passport[0]` | passport |
| 13. Travel Document Number | `Line14c_TravelDoc[0]` | usually blank |
| 14. Country of Issuance | `Line14d_CountryOfIssuance[0]` | passport |
| 15. Expiration Date for Passport | `Line14e_ExpDate[0]` | passport — **note `[0]` is item 15 and `[1]` is item 11.b, i.e. the reverse of reading order** |

`Not in U.S.` is 11 characters and fits the comb exactly. `Not in the U.S.` is 14

output that is easy to miss. Verified by render.

## Page 3 — Part 4 continued, Part 5 (about the petitioner)

### Part 4 continued — firm rule: English address, Chinese name, Chinese address

The three blocks on this page have fixed roles:

- **2.b** the applicant's **country of birth** (English)
- **3.a–3.f** that country-of-birth address, **English / pinyin**
- **4.a–4.c** the applicant's **Chinese name**
- **5.a–5.g** the **same address in Chinese characters**

The same foreign address therefore appears twice, romanised then in Chinese, with
the Chinese name between them. Filling Chinese characters into 3.a–3.f is the
usual way this goes wrong; check the alphabet on the render.

2.b's printed question is "current country of residence or, if now in the United
States, last country of permanent residence abroad". Country of birth and country
of residence coincide for nearly all of these clients, which is why the firm rule
is stated as country of birth — **if they differ, answer the printed question and
confirm with the attorney.**

follow that.

| 9.a–9.e Worksite | `Line9a_StreetNumberName[0]`, `Line9b_Unit[*]`/`Line9b_AptSteFlrNumber[0]`, `Line9c_CityOrTown[0]`, `Line9d_State[0]`, `Line9e_ZipCode[0]` | only if different from Part 1 |

On a CP case with no settled US job, the Chujun Yang precedent wrote
`To be determined` in 9.a and explained it in Part 11 against Page 4 / Part 6 /
Item 1 and 9. That is the pattern to follow — an empty Part 6 invites an RFE.

**A remote worker's worksite is their home address.** Short-form and film clients
in this practice are often fully remote, and the intake sheet then gives the
employer's office address (e.g. `remote (7080 Hollywood Blvd, Los Angeles)`), which
is *not* where the beneficiary works. Fill 9.a–9.e with the beneficiary's own
residential address and say so in Part 11 against Page 4 / Part 6 / Items 9.a–9.e.
This is not cosmetic: **the I-140 lockbox is chosen by the state the beneficiary
will work in**, so putting the employer's city there routes the package to the
wrong lockbox.

### Printed Part 11 — Additional Information (page 8, `Pt9Line*`)

Header, repeated from page 1: `Pt1Line1a_FamilyName[1]`,
`Pt1Line1b_GivenName[1]`, `Pt1Line1c_MiddleName[1]`,
`#area[6].Pt1Line3_TaxNumber[1]` (A-Number/EIN). **Easy to miss** after filling
page 1.

**Five** entry blocks, each a Page / Part / Item row above its text box. Ordered
as they appear on the page:

| Where | Page no. | Part no. | Item no. | Text box |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| left, middle | `Pt9Line3a_PageNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line3b_PartNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line3c_ItemNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line3d_AdditionalInfo[0]` |
| left, bottom | `Pt9Line4a_PageNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line4b_PartNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line4c_ItemNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line4d_AdditionalInfo[0]` |
| right, top | `Pt9Line5a_PageNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line5b_PartNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line5c_ItemNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line5d_AdditionalInfo[0]` |
| right, middle | `Pt9Line6a_PageNumber[1]` | `Pt9Line6b_PartNumber[1]` | `Pt9Line6c_ItemNumber[1]` | `Pt9Line6d_AdditionalInfo[0]` |
| right, bottom | `Pt9Line6a_PageNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line6b_PartNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line6c_ItemNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line6d_AdditionalInfo[1]` |

The two right-hand `Pt9Line6*` blocks are **crossed**: the `[1]` header row sits
above the `[0]` text box's neighbour, so the header labelled `[1]` belongs with
text `[0]` and the header labelled `[0]` belongs with text `[1]`. The top-left
position is occupied by the name/A-Number header, not by an entry block.

Typical uses on an EB-1A: the full romanised foreign address that would not fit in
Part 4 item 3.a, and an explanation of an unsettled US job.

**The text boxes are multiline but pypdf does not wrap for you** — it writes one long
line and everything past the right edge is invisible. Put explicit `\n` breaks in
the value, roughly 40 characters per line, and check the render.
