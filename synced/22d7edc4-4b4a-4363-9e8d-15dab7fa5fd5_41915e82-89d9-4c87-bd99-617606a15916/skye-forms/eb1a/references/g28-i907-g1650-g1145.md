# Field maps — G-28, I-907, G-1650, G-1145 (EB-1A / I-140 petitions)

Editions verified 2026-08-20 against each form's own USCIS page: G-28 **09/17/18**,
I-907 **04/01/24**, G-1650 **06/03/25**, G-1145 **09/26/14**. Re-verify with
`uscis_forms.py fetch g-28 i-907 g-1650 g-1145`.

These four forms are shared with the firm's O-1 work and the field maps are the
same. **What differs on an EB-1A is who the client is and what goes in the
"related case" boxes** — that is what this file records. Where a row here and the
`o1/references/` copy disagree, this one is the EB-1A answer.

F = FIXED firm data · S = self-petitioner (= beneficiary).

## I-907 — 04/01/24, 7 pages (only when premium processing is requested)

Prefix: `form1[0].#subform[N].`

### Part 1 — the person filing the request (pages 1–2)

| Item | Field | EB-1A value |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | `Pt1Line3_FamilyName[0]` / `Pt1Line3_GivenName[0]` | the attorney when the firm files; the self-petitioner when they file themselves |
| 4 | `Part1_Line4_CompanyorOrganizationName[0]` | blank on a self-petition — **all three company items (Part 1 item 4, Part 2 items 6 and 7) stay empty**, there is no company anywhere in an EB-1A self-petition |
| 5 | `Part1_Line5_MailingAddress_InCareofName[0]`, `_StreetNumberName[0]`, `_Unit[*]`, `_AptSteFlrNumber[0]`, `_CityTown[0]`, `_State[0]`, `_ZipCode[0]`, `_Country[0]` | filer's mailing address |
| 6 | `Part1Line6_Checkbox[0]`=`/Y` Yes / `[1]`=`/N` No | mailing address same as physical address — `/Y` when the firm files, and then Part 1 item **7** (physical address, page 2, `Part1_Line7_PhysicalAddress_*`) stays blank |
| — | `FormG28Attach[0]` = `/1` | only when a G-28 is attached |
| — | `AttyStateBarNumber[0]` | `348794`, G-28 cases only |
| 8 | `Part1_Line8_CheckBox` — `[2]`=`/A` · `[3]`=`/B` · `[0]`=`/C` · `[1]`=`/D` | see below |

**The four item-8 options are NOT in the order a naive text extraction suggests.**
Resolved by matching each printed label's y-position to the checkbox rect at the
same height:

| Export | Printed option |
|---|---|
| `/A` (`[2]`) | I am the **petitioner** |
| `/B` (`[3]`) | I am the attorney or accredited representative **for the petitioner** |
| `/C` (`[0]`) | I am the **applicant** |
| `/D` (`[1]`) | I am the attorney or accredited representative **for the applicant** |

An I-140 is a **petition**, so:

- firm files as attorney of record with a G-28 → **`/B`**
- the self-petitioner files for themselves, no G-28 → **`/A`** (what the Qing Yang
  precedent does, correctly)

`/C` and `/D` are for applications, not petitions. Never right here.

### Page 7 — Part 7 Additional Information

`Pt1Line3_FamilyName[1]` / `Pt1Line3_GivenName[1]` — filer's name repeated at
index `[1]`.

**Who signs it:** Part 3 is the *requestor's* signature, and with a G-28 the
requestor is the attorney (Part 1 is the attorney). So the I-907 adds nothing to the
client's signature packet — say so, because it looks like an omission otherwise.

### Two rules about who may file it

- **A beneficiary or co-applicant of the primary form may not file the I-907.** On
  a self-petition the beneficiary *is* the petitioner, so this is satisfied — but
  it is worth a sentence in the report, because it looks wrong at a glance.
- Premium processing on an I-140 puts the whole package in a **different lockbox**.
  See `fees-and-filing.md`.

## G-1650 — 06/03/25, 1 page, one per payment

Prefix: `form1[0].PG1[0].`

| Section | Field | Value | Src |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applicant/Petitioner | `sfApplicantPetitionerName[0].GivenName[0]` / `.FamilyName[0]` | the self-petitioner's name (all precedent G-1650s use it) | S |
| Account holder | `sfBankAccountHolderInfo[0].CB_AccountType[0]`=`/B` Business / `[1]`=`/P` Personal | `/B` when the firm pays | F |
| Account holder | `sfBankAccountHolderInfo[0].GivenName[0]` / `.FamilyName[0]` | `Shenqi` / `Cai` | F |
| Account info | `sfBankAccountInformation[0].BusinessName[0]` | `Law Office of Shenqi Cai APC` | F |
| Account info | `sfBankAccountInformation[0].CB_CreditCardType[1]`=`/V` Checking / `[0]`=`/MC` Savings | `/V` | F |
| Account info | `.BankName[0]` / `.RoutingNumber[0]` / `.AccountNumber[0]` | `Bank of America` / `121000358` / `325210063004` | F |
| Account info | `.AuthorizedPaymentAmt[0]` | the one fee this form pays, digits only (`715`, not `$715.00` — the form prints the `.00`) | — |
| Signature | `AuthorizationandSignature[0].SignatureOfApplicant[0]` | leave blank | — |

`CB_CreditCardType` is misleadingly named: it is the Checking/Savings pair, not a
card type. `/V` is the leftmost box, Checking.

**Who pays decides how much of this gets filled, and it forks on the G-28.**

| | **with G-28** (firm pays) | **without G-28** (client pays) |
|---|---|---|
| What to fill | everything: applicant name, `/B` Business, holder `Shenqi` / `Cai`, business name, `/V` Checking, bank name, routing, account, amount | **the amount only** — one number per form (`715`, `300`, `2965`) |
| Who completes the rest | nobody, it is done | the client, by hand or in Acrobat: their own name, account type, bank name, routing, account, and the signature |

Firm instruction, 2026-08: on a no-G-28 case the G-1650s go out with **only the
three amounts** filled. The client's bank details are not ours to type, and the
form is signed by the account holder — them. That also explains the Qing Yang
precedent, whose three G-1650s carry only a name and the amount: it was deliberate,
not an omission.

Say it plainly when handing these over: **an amount-only G-1650 is not payable
yet.** If the client mails it as-is, USCIS cannot pull the money and rejects the
whole package.

## G-1145 — 09/26/14, 1 page

Prefix: `form1[0].#subform[0].`

| Field | EB-1A value |
|---|---|
| `LastName[0]` / `FirstName[0]` / `MiddleName[0]` | the client's name |
| `Email[0]` | `cassie@lingtulaw.com` on the with-G-28 precedent — a firm-monitored mailbox, not the client's |
| `MobilePhoneNumber[0]` | `4155479999` on the with-G-28 precedent — the firm's number |

The e-notification goes wherever this form says, so with a G-28 it points at the
firm and the client gets told about acceptance by the firm.

**Only the with-G-28 precedent has a G-1145 at all** — Qing Yang's no-G-28 folder
has none. Whether a self-filing client should get one with their own email, or
none, is a firm decision that the two precedents do not settle. Ask, and record
the answer here.
