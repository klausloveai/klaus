# Field map — I-140 (EB-2 NIW self-petition)

Edition **06/07/24**, 8 pages, 286 AcroForm fields. Verified 2026-08-24 against
`uscis.gov/i-140` (page Edition Date 06/07/24, footer 06/07/24 — they agree) and
by rendering every page of the blank form. Re-verify with `forms.py fetch i-140`
before reuse.

Prefix for every name on page *N*: `form1[0].#subform[N-1].` — e.g. page 2 fields
are `form1[0].#subform[1].<name>`. The tables below give the bare name.

F = FIXED firm data · S = self-petitioner (= beneficiary on an NIW) · D = dependent.

**What is different from the firm's EB-1A I-140** — read this if you already know
that work, because three things move:

| | EB-1A | **EB-2 NIW** |
|---|---|---|
| Part 2 petition type | `/a` extraordinary ability | **`/h`** — "An alien applying for an NIW" |
| Page 2 item 11.b | the I-94 "Admit Until Date" | the I-94 Admit Until Date, but **`D/S`** when the status is F-1 or J-1 |
| Part 5 items 2–8, Part 6 | mostly blank / `To be determined` | actually filled — an NIW client normally has a real US job |

Everything else — the offsets, the Province/Postal inversion, the page-3
three-block rule, the CP default — is the same form and the same firm policy.

## Why the names never match the printed numbers

The AcroForm names were carried over from an older layout, so on this edition they
are systematically offset from what is printed on the page. Four separate offsets:

| Block | Offset | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Part 3 "Other Information" (p2) | names run **+2** | printed **3.** Date of Birth = `Line5_DateOfBirth` |
| Part 3 last-arrival block (p2) | names run **−1** | printed **11.a** I-94 number = `Line14a_ArrivalDeparture` |
| every address block | letters offset | printed **3.a** street (p1) = `Line6b_StreetNumberName` |
| Province / Postal Code, all blocks | **swapped** | printed **3.g** Province = `Line6h_Province` |

**Length limits measured on this edition** (verified 2026-08-25 by reading `/MaxLen`
off the widgets and by rendering): page 2 item 11.a `MaxLen 11` · page 3 item 3.a
`MaxLen 34` · page 4 Part 6 item 1 Job Title `MaxLen 31` · page 4 item 9.a
`MaxLen 34` · page 6 printed Part 8 item 5 Email `MaxLen 30`. Page 3 item 3.c has
no `MaxLen` but visually clips near 30 characters, which is worse — the value is
stored in full and only the render shows the loss.
| printed Parts 8–11 (p6–p8) | prefix runs **−1 Part** | printed **Part 8** = `Part7_*` fields |

Resolve anything not listed here by rect with
`forms.py dump forms/i-140.pdf --pages N`, against the rendered page — never by
reading the field name.

### Part 2 — Petition Type (item 1 continues onto page 2)

One checkbox, `prt2PetitionType`, eight members:

| Export | Index | Printed option |
|---|---|---|
| `/a` | `[1]` | 1.a. An alien of extraordinary ability (EB-1A) |
| `/b` | `[2]` | 1.b. An outstanding professor or researcher |
| `/c` | `[3]` | 1.c. A multinational executive or manager |
| `/d` | `[4]` | 1.d. Advanced degree / exceptional ability, **NOT** seeking NIW |
| `/e` | `[0]` | 1.e. A professional (bachelor's or foreign equivalent) |
| `/f` | `[7]` | 1.f. A skilled worker (≥2 years) — page 2 |
| `/g` | `[5]` | 1.g. Any other worker (<2 years) — page 2 |
| **`/h`** | `[6]` | **1.h. An alien applying for an NIW ← this is the NIW box** |

firm's NIW Checklist calls it out as "Page 2, Part 2, Q1h: Yes?".

exceptional ability, and it is the box an NIW client's classification would
otherwise fit — but it reads "who is **NOT** seeking a National Interest Waiver".
Ticking `/d` on an NIW petition asks for the wrong thing.

Also on page 2: 2.a amend a previous petition `Line2_Petition[0]`=`/A` with
`Line2a_ReceiptNumber[0]`; 2.b Schedule A `Line2_Petition[1]`=`/S`. Both blank on
a normal NIW.

### Part 3 — Information About His or Her Last Arrival in the United States

Printed items 10 through 11.c. **This block is filled from the client's I-94, and
only when the client is inside the US.** The form says so itself: "If the person
for whom you are filing is in the United States, provide the following
information."

| Client | 10. Date of Last Arrival | 11.a. I-94 number | 11.b. Authorized stay expires | 11.c. Status on I-94 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **in the US** | from the I-94 | from the I-94 | from the I-94 | from the I-94 |
| **not in the US** | blank | **`Not in U.S.`** | blank | blank |

| Printed | Field | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 10. Date of Last Arrival | `Line13_DateOArrival[0]` | I-94 admission date |
| **11.a. Form I-94 Arrival-Departure Record Number** | `Line14_I94Number[0].Line14a_ArrivalDeparture[0]` | **comb field, `MaxLen 11`** |
| **11.b. Expiration Date of Authorized Stay** | `Line14e_ExpDate[1]` | the I-94 "Admit Until Date" — which on an **F-1 or J-1** I-94 reads **`D/S`**, not a date |
| 11.c. Status on Form I-94 | `Line15_CurrentNon[0]` | I-94 class of admission, e.g. `H1B`, `F1`, `B2` |
| 12. Passport Number | `Line14b_Passport[0]` | passport |
| 13. Travel Document Number | `Line14c_TravelDoc[0]` | usually blank |
| 14. Country of Issuance | `Line14d_CountryOfIssuance[0]` | passport |
| 15. Expiration Date for Passport | `Line14e_ExpDate[0]` | passport — **note `[0]` is item 15 and `[1]` is item 11.b, i.e. the reverse of reading order** |

Three things about this block, each of which has gone wrong on a filed form:

- **Item 11.b on an F-1 or J-1 is `D/S`.** That is what a duration-of-status I-94
  actually shows, and the firm's NIW Checklist states it as a rule ("Page 2, Part
  3, Q11b: If F1 or J1 status, write 'D/S' instead of specific date"). Substituting
  a date — an I-20 program end date, an OPT end date — misstates the record.
- **The value for 11.a when the client is abroad is `Not in U.S.`, exactly 11
  characters.** Item 11.a is a comb field with `MaxLen 11`, verified on the current
  blank. `not in the U.S.` is 15 characters: the fill warns once, stores the full
  string, and the printed appearance shows `n o t  i n  t h e` with the `U.S.`
  gone. Verified by rendering both. If different wording is wanted, count the
  characters first — 11 cells is the hard limit.
- **Items 12–15 are outside the conditional block.** Passport number, travel
  document, country of issuance and expiry are filled either way, from the
  passport. The passport number at item 12 is a commonly missed box.

**Do not carry a stale entry into this block.** A client living abroad who once
visited on a B-2 that has since expired is *not in the US*: item 11.a reads
`Not in U.S.` and 10, 11.b and 11.c stay blank. Filling the block from an expired
admission has reached a filed form and is the wrong answer, not a shortcut.

### Part 4 — Processing Information (firm default: always CP, Guangzhou, China)

| Printed | Field | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 1.a. Alien will apply for a visa abroad at a U.S. Embassy or Consulate at: | `Line1a_Visa[0]` | `/1a` |
| 1.b. City or Town | `Line1a_CityorTown[0]` | `Guangzhou` |
| 1.c. Country | `Line1a_Country[0]` | `China` |
| 2.a. Alien is in the US and will apply for adjustment of status | `Line1b_Status[0]` (`/1b`) | leave off |

Mutually exclusive, and **CP is the default** even when the beneficiary is sitting
in the US on an H-1B. The Checklist states it as a thing to verify: "Page 2, Part
4, Q1a: Yes? Guangzhou, China?". Depart from it only on an explicit instruction
from the attorney, and if you do, say so in the report.

**Fill all three boxes.** Ticking `/1a` and leaving 1.b and 1.c empty — a consular
box with no consulate named — has reached a filed form, as has leaving the whole
of item 1 blank.

**Consequence:** on a CP case the dependents in Part 7 answer "applying for
adjustment of status" = No and "applying for a visa abroad" = Yes.

### Where to get the Chinese — the intake sheet does not ask for it

**This is the single most likely block to ship blank, and blank is wrong.** The
form's condition is "If the person's native alphabet is other than Roman letters"
— for a Chinese national that is always true, so 4.a–4.c and 5.a–5.g are not
optional. But the firm's intake questionnaire has **no field for 中文姓名 and no
field for the Chinese form of the address**: it collects the romanised name and
the China address in pinyin only. So the facts you need are, by construction, not
in the sheet you are filling from, and it is easy to record "not provided" and move
on. Don't.

Look for them in this order:

1. **The Chinese-language material already in the case folder.** These clients
   almost always have some, and it carries the legal characters:
   - 软著 (software copyright) application or registration folders — often *named*
     after the 著作权人
   - Chinese news coverage in the 新闻 folder — a bylined article names the subject
     in Chinese, and a published third-party source is good evidence
   - signed Chinese contracts, MOUs, purchase orders — 法定代表人 / 授权代表 blocks
   - 证书 / 奖状 issued in China
   - the passport's Chinese-character name line, if the scan is in the folder
2. **Two independent sources before you write it.** A single folder name is a hint,
   not a source. A folder name plus a published article is enough. One document
   plus your own guess is not.
3. **If it is not in the folder, ask the client** — and add it to the intake
   request rather than leaving the boxes empty.

**Never transliterate pinyin back into characters yourself.** `Su` could be 苏, 素,
塑, 速, 粟; `Jingnansan Rd` could be 京南三路 or 经南三路. Chinese homophones make
this a coin flip, and a wrong character is a wrong legal name or a wrong address on
a federal petition. If one component of an address is documented and another is not,
fill what is documented, leave the undocumented box empty, and say which character
you need confirmed — a partly-filled Chinese address with a named gap is honest; an
invented one is not.

### These fields must go through `cjk_stamp.py`, not `fill`

The I-140's text fields carry a Helvetica `/DA`, which has no CJK glyphs. Passing
Chinese through `forms.py fill` stores the right value and renders every character
as `?`. So split the values:

- ASCII and digits in the 5.a–5.g block (the Apt/Ste/Flr tick, the unit number, the
  postal code) go through `fill` like anything else.
- The characters — 4.a–4.c, and 5.b/5.d/5.e/5.g — go through `cjk_stamp.py` in a
  second pass, and must **not** also be set by `fill`, or `?` is drawn on top of the
  stamp.

Then **render and read the characters back**. The stamp is page content, not a form
value, so `dump --filled-only` will not show it — a failed stamp looks like an empty
box, and only the render tells you.

**Items 9 and 10 are about a certified labor certification, which an NIW does not
have.** The waiver is precisely a waiver of the job offer and the labor
certification, so both answers are No — but they are No for a *reason*, not by
default, and the ETA-9089 the package carries is an **uncertified** one filed to
document the classification. Do not let the presence of a 9089 in the folder talk
you into a Yes.

Items 7 and 8 are real questions about the client's history. **Item 8 matters on a
re-file**: a second NIW petition after a first was denied answers **Yes**, with the
earlier receipt number, office and outcome written into Part 11. Ask whether this
is a re-file rather than defaulting to No.

**The SOC code and job title must agree with ETA-9089 Appendix A's item 1g and the
Final Determination's items 8–10**, because both documents travel in the same
envelope and describe the same job. Cross-check them against each other, not just
against the intake sheet.

**Wages at item 8 should match Part 5 item 12 Annual Income** unless there is a
reason they differ. A filed form has an income and a wage that agree with each
other but not with the intake sheet — take the number from a document, not from
another box on the same form.

**A remote or hybrid worker's worksite is their own home address.** An intake sheet
that answers "worksite" with the employer's offices in two cities has not given a
worksite. This is not cosmetic: **the I-140 lockbox is chosen by the state the
beneficiary will work in**, so putting the employer's city there routes the package
to the wrong lockbox. Fill 9.a–9.e with the beneficiary's own address and explain
it in Part 11.

### Printed Part 11 — Additional Information (page 8, `Pt9Line*`)

Header, repeated from page 1: `Pt1Line1a_FamilyName[1]`,
`Pt1Line1b_GivenName[1]`, `Pt1Line1c_MiddleName[1]`,
`#area[6].Pt1Line3_TaxNumber[1]` (A-Number/EIN). **This header is the single most
commonly missed thing on the form** — it is on a page that is often left blank
entirely. Fill it.

**Five** entry blocks, each a Page / Part / Item row above its text box:

| Where | Page no. | Part no. | Item no. | Text box |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| left, middle | `Pt9Line3a_PageNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line3b_PartNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line3c_ItemNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line3d_AdditionalInfo[0]` |
| left, bottom | `Pt9Line4a_PageNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line4b_PartNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line4c_ItemNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line4d_AdditionalInfo[0]` |
| right, top | `Pt9Line5a_PageNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line5b_PartNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line5c_ItemNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line5d_AdditionalInfo[0]` |
| right, middle | `Pt9Line6a_PageNumber[1]` | `Pt9Line6b_PartNumber[1]` | `Pt9Line6c_ItemNumber[1]` | `Pt9Line6d_AdditionalInfo[0]` |
| right, bottom | `Pt9Line6a_PageNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line6b_PartNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line6c_ItemNumber[0]` | `Pt9Line6d_AdditionalInfo[1]` |

The two right-hand `Pt9Line6*` blocks are **crossed**: the header labelled `[1]`
belongs with text `[0]` and the header labelled `[0]` belongs with text `[1]`. The
top-left position is occupied by the name/A-Number header, not by an entry block.

Typical NIW uses: the full romanised foreign address that would not fit in Part 4
item 3.a; an explanation of a remote or hybrid worksite; and on a re-file, the
prior petition's receipt number and outcome against Page 3 / Part 4 / Item 8.

**The text boxes are multiline but pypdf does not wrap** — it writes one long line
and everything past the right edge is invisible. Put explicit `\n` breaks in the
value, roughly 40 characters per line, and check the render.
