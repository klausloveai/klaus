# Field maps — G-28, I-907, G-1650, G-1145 (EB-2 NIW petitions)

Editions verified 2026-08-24 against each form's own USCIS page: G-28 **09/17/18**,
I-907 **04/01/24**, G-1650 **06/03/25**, G-1145 **09/26/14**. Re-verify with
`forms.py fetch g-28 i-907 g-1650 g-1145`.

These four forms are shared with the firm's O-1 and EB-1A work and the field maps
are the same. **What differs on an NIW is what goes in the "related case" boxes** —
that is what this file records. Where a row here and the
`eb1a/references/` or `o1/references/` copy disagree, this one is the NIW answer.

F = FIXED firm data · S = self-petitioner (= beneficiary).

officer notices.

G-28 while the I-140 in the same package had it right, which is why the firm's NIW
Checklist opens every form's section with "Name order: Last Name, First Name".
Check all four forms, not one.

**Set only `Line4_Checkbox[3]`.** A filed G-28 sets all five members to `/P`; it
renders as Petitioner only because the other four boxes' on-states do not match
`/P`.

## I-907 — 04/01/24, 7 pages (only when premium processing is requested)

Prefix: `form1[0].#subform[N].`

| Item | Field | NIW value |
|---|---|---|

| 5 | `Part1_Line5_MailingAddress_InCareofName[0]`, `_StreetNumberName[0]`, `_Unit[*]`, `_AptSteFlrNumber[0]`, `_CityTown[0]`, `_State[0]`, `_ZipCode[0]`, `_Country[0]` | filer's mailing address — the firm's with a G-28, the client's without |

| — | `AttyStateBarNumber[0]` | `348794`, G-28 cases only |
| 8 | `Part1_Line8_CheckBox` — `[2]`=`/A` · `[3]`=`/B` · `[0]`=`/C` · `[1]`=`/D` | see below |

**The four item-8 options are NOT in the order a naive text extraction suggests.**
Resolved by matching each printed label's y-position to the checkbox rect at the
same height:

| Export | Printed option |
|---|---|
| `/A` (`[2]`) | I am the **petitioner** |
| `/B` (`[3]`) | I am the attorney or accredited representative **for the petitioner** |
| `/C` (`[0]`) | I am the **applicant** |
| `/D` (`[1]`) | I am the attorney or accredited representative **for the applicant** |

An I-140 is a **petition**, so:

- firm files as attorney of record with a G-28 → **`/B`**
- the self-petitioner files for themselves, no G-28 → **`/A`**

`/C` and `/D` are for applications, not petitions. Never right here.

**Items 6 and 7 for a client living abroad**: item 6 = **No**, the mailing address
is the US address they are reachable at, and item 7 is the actual foreign
residence — with the State box left empty and Province used instead. That is how
you say "reachable at a US address, actually living abroad".

**Set one box at item 6.** A filed I-907 ticks both Yes and No there, and then
gives a physical address one street number away from its own mailing address.

### Page 7 — Part 7 Additional Information

`Pt1Line3_FamilyName[1]` / `Pt1Line3_GivenName[1]` — filer's name repeated at
index `[1]`. Commonly left blank; fill it.

**Who signs it:** Part 3 is the *requestor's* signature, and with a G-28 the
requestor is the attorney. So on a G-28 case the I-907 adds nothing to the client's
signature packet — say so, because it looks like an omission otherwise. Without a
G-28 the client signs it, and the firm's Checklist requires that signature to be
clear, handwritten and dated.

**A beneficiary or co-applicant of the primary form may not file the I-907.** On a
self-petition the beneficiary *is* the petitioner, so this is satisfied — worth a
sentence in the report, because it looks wrong at a glance.

## G-1650 — 06/03/25, 1 page, one per payment

Prefix: `form1[0].PG1[0].`

**Who pays forks on the G-28, and it decides how much of this form gets filled.**

| Section | Field | **with G-28** | **without G-28** | Src |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Applicant/Petitioner | `sfApplicantPetitionerName[0].GivenName[0]` / `.FamilyName[0]` | the self-petitioner's name | the self-petitioner's name | S |
| Account holder | `sfBankAccountHolderInfo[0].CB_AccountType[0]`=`/B` Business / `[1]`=`/P` Personal | `/B` | **blank** | F |
| Account holder | `sfBankAccountHolderInfo[0].GivenName[0]` / `.FamilyName[0]` | `Shenqi` / `Cai` | **blank** | F |
| Account info | `sfBankAccountInformation[0].BusinessName[0]` | `Law Office of Shenqi Cai APC` | **blank** | F |
| Account info | `sfBankAccountInformation[0].CB_CreditCardType[1]`=`/V` Checking / `[0]`=`/MC` Savings | `/V` | **blank** | F |
| Account info | `.BankName[0]` / `.RoutingNumber[0]` / `.AccountNumber[0]` | `Bank of America` / `121000358` / `325210063004` | **blank** | F |
| Account info | `.AuthorizedPaymentAmt[0]` | the one fee this form pays, digits only (`715`, not `$715.00` — the form prints the `.00`) | same | — |
| Signature | `AuthorizationandSignature[0].SignatureOfApplicant[0]` | leave blank | leave blank | — |

**Firm rule (confirmed 2026-08-25): on a no-G-28 case the G-1650 carries only the
applicant's name and the amount. The whole payment method is left blank.** The
firm is not attorney of record, so the client is filing and paying, and the firm's
ACH account has no business appearing on a form the client submits. Account type,
account-holder name, business name, Checking/Savings, bank name, routing number and
account number all stay empty for the client to complete.

That means a no-G-28 G-1650 leaves the firm's hands **incomplete on purpose**, and
it is not a defect to fix — but it *is* something to say out loud in the hand-off,
because an incomplete bank block is otherwise indistinguishable from an omission.
The three G-1650s go to the client alongside the signature pages, and USCIS will
reject the filing if the client submits them with the bank block still empty. Say
so in the report.

With a G-28 the firm pays from the account above and the block is filled in full.

`CB_CreditCardType` is misleadingly named: it is the Checking/Savings pair, not a
card type. `/V` is the leftmost box, Checking.

**The firm's earlier NIW filings did not use a G-1650 at all** — the client Zelled
the fee to the firm and the firm wrote a check, which is the flow the NIW Checklist
still describes. **The firm's instruction as of 2026-08-24 is that NIW filings use
G-1650 ACH**, one form per payment, on the firm account above. If a case is to be
paid by check instead, that is a departure from the standing instruction — confirm
it and note it in the report.

**A G-1650 with an empty bank block is not mailable** — USCIS cannot pull the money
and the whole package is rejected. On a no-G-28 case that is exactly the state the
form leaves the firm in, by design; the client has to complete it before filing.

## G-1145 — 09/26/14, 1 page

Prefix: `form1[0].#subform[0].`

| Field | NIW value |
|---|---|
| `LastName[0]` / `FirstName[0]` / `MiddleName[0]` | the client's name |
| `Email[0]` | `cassie@lingtulaw.com` on a G-28 case — a firm-monitored mailbox |
| `MobilePhoneNumber[0]` | `4155479999` on a G-28 case |

**Two firm rules, both from the NIW Checklist, and neither is obvious:**

1. **G-1145 is filed only when premium processing is NOT requested.** The
   Checklist's item 5 is headed "Form G-1145 (No Premium Processing)", and on a
   filed premium case that whole section is struck through. The logic is that a
   premium case gets its acceptance news through the 15-day clock anyway. So the
   G-1145 and the I-907 are mutually exclusive in a standard package.
2. **When it is filed, it goes on top** — "Put this as the first page of the
   package."

Because the firm's filed NIW cases all used premium, there is no worked G-1145
example to copy. On a no-premium case with no G-28, ask whose email and phone go
on it.
